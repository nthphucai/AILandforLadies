paper_title: "Assessing the Role of Subsurface Hydrology in Landslide Initiation and Slope Stability in Tropical Montane Regions"

authors: ["Dr. Elena Petrova", "Prof. Ricardo Morales", "Nguyen Thi Minh, PhD", "Dr. David Kim"]

summary: This research investigates the complex interplay between subsurface hydrological processes and the mechanisms triggering landslides in vulnerable tropical montane environments. Focusing on selected sites in the Central Highlands of Vietnam and the Cordillera Central of the Philippines, the study employs a combination of in-situ hydro-geological monitoring (pore water pressure, soil moisture content), geophysical surveys (electrical resistivity tomography, seismic refraction), and physically-based slope stability modeling. The aim is to quantify how variations in rainfall intensity and duration influence groundwater levels and pore water pressure, leading to critical reductions in shear strength and subsequent slope failure. Findings from this study are crucial for developing more accurate early warning systems, informing land-use planning, and designing effective mitigation strategies to reduce the devastating impact of landslides on local communities and infrastructure in these high-risk regions.
